# Simulated focus group

These are structured reviews by role. They are not interviews with real people.

Repeated across the professional players, the teacher, the engineer, and both design roles:

- Trust is the click staying put for a long practice, including after a tempo change and after the phone locks.
- Play and Stop have to be obvious with a glance and a sweaty finger.
- Accent, meter, and subdivision have to be reachable with the other hand on an instrument.
- The current faces still look drawn. A drummer, guitarist, and industrial designer all describe the same gap: the object does not look machined, mounted, or lit by one source.
- Extra features lose to a faster path back to tempo and play. The phrase deck and tuner are secondary. They must leave the click alone when dismissed.
- Bluetooth is expected to be late. The app should not pretend a visual beat corrects that.
- Small targets and look-alike controls are the deletion risk, ahead of missing DAW features.

Used: the Play mark is now a cut in the brass bar, so stopped and playing read as different objects in the new stills. The closed shutter is a stack of slats. Pro cells share one tray.

Still repeated, and not solved: Rhythm, Pulse, Pendulum, and Beatline still look drawn. Pulse still reads as arcs around a mark. The pendulum is still a bar, a block, and a plate. Beatline nodes are still capsules; the reconstruction test requires `borderRadius: grown / 2`, so that shape was put back after a squarer pass failed the suite. The simulated players would still not trust the object as machined hardware.


Not used: requests for polyrhythm, setlists, swing, coach mode, or a second timing display. Those add a second authority or a second product.
